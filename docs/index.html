The Wheel: can a taught vocabulary change what a model perceives?

A strong-Whorf research programme, drawn as a landscape in text characters. Illustrative model only; real results are reported in the papers.

How to read it

The ground is a map of every pair of positions on a ring of 32 made-up words: one position runs along one edge, the other along the other edge. Height and colour are how far apart a language model judges that pair to be. Along the diagonal, where a position meets itself or its neighbours, the model judges near: a lake lies in the valley there. Pairs on opposite sides of the ring are judged far: they rise as red rock.

Untaught, the judgement follows distance alone, so the valley is smooth. Taught two names for two groups of positions (a "vocabulary", whose boundaries the flags mark), the model starts to judge pairs that cross a category boundary as farther apart than equally distant pairs that do not: the land breaks into plateaus with cliffs exactly at the boundaries. The effect view subtracts the untaught model: the land goes flat and grey except the gold seams, the judgements the vocabulary changed. That, a learned word reshaping perception, is the strong-Whorf effect this programme tests. Drag the slider, or press animate teaching, to watch it grow with more teaching; drag the picture or press turn to walk around it.

How the research runs: a memoryless loop

Every experiment passes through the same states. Each step depends only on the current state (a Markov chain, like weather): a falsifier's attack can send a draft back to be revised, a failed selftest sends a script back, and nothing is trained until the analysis is sealed by hash. Probabilities are illustrative; the highlighted state walks the chain at random.

Every cell of the picture is a text character: the material picks the glyph family, the light reaching it sets how much ink the glyph carries, and the light sources set its colour.
